## Role

You are a helpful assistant answering with a single ordinary call. There is
no structured validation, no critic, and no deterministic validator in this
arm. Your prose numbers are never used as scores; Python scores your
selection afterwards against hidden labels you cannot see.

## Task

Rank the hidden candidate items and select exactly {budget_count} of them
that you expect to have the highest mean Bliss excess, defined below.

## Definitions (identical for both arms)

- An item is one ordered (NSC1, NSC2, CELLNAME) group with a complete
  dose grid of 9 or 15 points.
- For one dose point, the per-point difference is
  EXPECTEDGROWTH − PERCENTGROWTH, where EXPECTEDGROWTH is the
  Bliss-expected percent growth and PERCENTGROWTH is the observed
  percent growth with time-zero correction.
- An item's observed_score is the unrounded mean of its per-point
  differences. A positive score means average observed growth below
  Bliss-expected growth (greater-than-additive on average).
- You are given a train table of items with their observed_scores and a
  candidate list of hidden items WITHOUT any outcome values. Use only
  the information given. Do not invent an item, a score, or an outcome.

## Inputs you receive

1. The train item table (train_items.csv): item_id, NSC1, NSC2, cell
   line, grid size, observed_score, per-point differences.
2. The hidden candidate list (hidden_candidates.csv): item_id, NSC1,
   NSC2, cell line, grid size, concentration values. No outcome columns.
3. The budget count for this run: {budget_count}.

## Output (only this JSON, no other text)

```json
{"selected_item_ids": ["<item_id>", "..."]}
```

Rules for the output:

- Select exactly {budget_count} item ids.
- Every selected id must come from the hidden candidate list.
- No duplicates.
- If you cannot decide, still select exactly {budget_count} ids from
  the candidate list using the train table as best you can. Declining
  to select is recorded as a none result with zero hits.
